The range

Four materials, four markets

Each raw material serves a distinct buyer and price point — from temple prasadam trays to resort dinnerware. Tap a card to open its full profile.

Areca Palm ₹2–24 / pc

Heat-pressed from naturally fallen palm sheaths — the flagship material.

+ Full profile
  • Strong, rigid & leak-proof — holds hot, oily and liquid foods; soup-tested up to 4 hours.
  • Heat-safe to ~180°C — microwave and oven friendly.
  • Composts in 60–90 days — no bleach, wax or resin added.
  • Premium wood-grain finish — upscale look for weddings & catering.
WeddingsCateringWholesale retailModerate cost

Sal / Siali Leaf ₹1–3 / pc

Hand-stitched Shorea & Siali leaves — the traditional, artisan option.

+ Full profile
  • Lightweight & flexible — sewn together by rural artisans.
  • Fastest to compost — biodegrades in roughly 45–60 days.
  • Leaks with liquids — moderate heat resistance; best kept to dry or semi-dry food.
  • Rustic, cultural appeal — the classic plate of temples and street food.
Temple prasadamDry snacksStreet foodNot for soups

Sugarcane Bagasse ₹2–10 / pc

Molded from pulped sugarcane residue — the workhorse for delivery & takeout.

+ Full profile
  • Highly grease-resistant — sturdy for both hot and cold foods.
  • Low cost at volume — ideal for fast-casual outlets and food delivery.
  • Needs industrial composting — doesn't break down in a home heap.
  • Utilitarian matte look — lacks the "craft" appeal of true leafware.
DeliveryTakeoutFast-casualIndustrial compost only

Bamboo Fiber ₹15–40 / pc

Crafted from bamboo pulp & strips — the luxury tier for resorts.

+ Full profile
  • Excellent strength with a genuinely premium look and feel.
  • Reusable for limited use — strongly eco-branded.
  • Expensive & heavier — a niche, high-end option rather than a volume seller.
  • Best placed in upscale events, resorts and gifting.
Upscale eventsResortsHigh price point

First 12 months

From first pallet to first plant

Distribution first, brand second, region third, manufacturing last — each phase funds the next.

Months 1–3 · Distribution setup

Source, certify, sample

  • Sign volume contracts with regional manufacturers (KRR, Halo, Hanuma, Haripriya…)
  • Apply for FSSAI licence; begin BIS IS 18267:2023 alignment
  • Build sample kits across 4″–12″ plates, bowls and spoons
  • Run first B2B pilots with Hyderabad caterers, hotels and temples
Months 4–6 · Brand launch

Green, clean and visible

  • Finalise brand identity — leaf-forward packaging, certificates on display
  • Launch website and social profiles with product photos and the sustainability story
  • Attend eco-friendly and catering expos; pitch hotel associations
  • Collect testimonials from local eco-enthusiasts and chefs; offer intro discounts
Months 7–9 · Regional expansion

Open the corridors

  • Set up logistics lanes: Hyderabad → Vijayawada, Pune, Bangalore
  • Stock Hyderabad wholesale markets; add custom logo-printing for large orders
  • List on e-commerce marketplaces; partner with organic stores and party-supply shops
  • Tie up with wellness events, eco-tourism resorts and green food festivals
Months 10–12 · Scale & assess manufacturing

Deeper roots

  • Enter Karnataka & Kerala — areca plate use is native to the Udupi belt
  • Complete ISO 17088 compostability certification and CPCB claim registration
  • Evaluate an in-house processing plant in Telangana's rural leaf belts
  • Apply Make-in-India green manufacturing incentives to lower capex
